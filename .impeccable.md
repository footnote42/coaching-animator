# Impeccable Design Context — Coaching Animator

## Design Context

### Users

Grassroots rugby coaches: club coaches, PE teachers, weekend volunteers. Time-poor by definition — they squeeze session planning into evenings and use the tool at the pitch. Technically ordinary: if WhatsApp is their benchmark, complexity is the enemy. They consume plays on a phone in daylight, outdoors, often mid-session with gloves on.

**Primary device split**: desktop to *create*, mobile to *view*. The landing page and gallery are frequently experienced on a phone. The editor is primarily desktop.

**Job to be done**: communicate a play to their players faster than a verbal explanation. The tool works if a player can understand the play just from watching the animation.

### Brand Personality

Three words: **direct · tactical · grassroots**

- **Direct**: no marketing fluff, no corporate polish, no animations that delay the message. Say the thing.
- **Tactical**: references the coaching whiteboard tradition — hand-drawn diagrams, coaching notepads, printed match programmes. Authentic to how rugby coaching actually looks and feels.
- **Grassroots**: community-owned, volunteer-built spirit. Not a VC-backed SaaS. Something a club coach would trust rather than feel intimidated by.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Primary reference**: the coaching whiteboard and the printed coaching clipboard. Not digitised — *referencing* it. The physicality of a marker on a whiteboard, a play printed on a laminated card, a rugby programme from the 1980s with its utilitarian typography and deliberate ink weight.

**Anti-references** (what it must not look like):
- Generic SaaS product pages (Notion, Linear, Vercel-style marketing)
- Corporate sports analytics dashboards (IBM, SAP Sports)
- AI-generated "modern" UIs (cyan accents on dark, glassmorphism, gradient text)
- Consumer fitness apps (Strava, Nike Run Club — too lifestyle, too personal)

**Color**: Palette is open for rethinking. The dark pitch green (#1A3D1A) and warm amber (#D97706) are in the right *territory* (serious, sport-specific, no-nonsense) but execution can evolve. Any revised palette should:
- Feel sport-specific, not startup-generic
- Work at WCAG AA contrast ratios
- Have a dominant neutral that feels like a coaching document (cream, aged-white, or off-white — not pure white or pure black)
- Reserve the accent (amber or equivalent) for singular calls to action — it must be rare to be effective

**Theme**: Light. Coaching is a daytime, outdoor activity. The dark hero sections are a useful contrast band, not the overall theme.

**Typography**: Current Inter usage is a gap — Inter is the monoculture default. Future typography work should find a font with more character that references printed coaching materials or editorial sports journalism. Display font should have deliberate ink weight. Body font should prioritise legibility on small mobile screens at arm's length outdoors.

**Border radius**: 0px (zero) is a deliberate decision — it reinforces the utilitarian, printed quality. Maintain this.

### Design Principles

1. **Analog authenticity over digital polish** — every design decision should ask "does this feel like something physical?" A coaching clipboard, a printed programme, a whiteboard. Avoid anything that reads as generic software.

2. **No cognitive load, ever** — coaches have 30 seconds of attention between whistle blows. One clear action per screen. Labels that say what they mean. No hover-to-reveal critical information.

3. **Rugby-specific, not sport-generic** — colors, imagery, and copy should feel specific to rugby coaching, not interchangeable with football or basketball tools. The field shapes, the team terminology, the tone of voice all reinforce the specificity.

4. **Mobile-first consumption, desktop-first creation** — the gallery and share/replay views must be beautiful and fingertip-usable on mobile; the editor accepts desktop complexity. These are different design contexts — don't compromise one for the other.

5. **Community over brand** — the gallery should feel like a shared coaching resource, not a product showcase. The tool should feel like it belongs to the coaches who use it, not to a company.

### Priority Areas

1. **Landing page** — first impression for coaches discovering the tool; must immediately communicate value to a time-poor rugby coach
2. **Gallery / Browse** — community playbook experience; must feel worth browsing, not generic

### Accessibility

WCAG AA as the baseline. 4.5:1 minimum contrast for all body text. Keyboard-navigable. Focus indicators visible. Reduced-motion respected for any animations.
