# Coaching Pedagogy & Frameworks

**Context**: Strategic exploration for coaching-animator v2+. These frameworks provide the pedagogical foundation that elevates the tool from "animation editor" to "coaching education platform."

---

## APES Framework

**Acronym**: Active, Purposeful, Enjoyable, Safe

**Purpose**: Minimum standard for any coaching session or drill. Ensures holistic player development.

- **Active**: Players are engaged, moving, making decisions
- **Purposeful**: Every drill has clear learning outcomes aligned to match demands
- **Enjoyable**: Intrinsic motivation; players want to come back
- **Safe**: Physical and psychological safety; no fear of failure

**Application in coaching-animator**: Coaches could tag drills with APES alignment, helping them design sessions that hit all four dimensions.

---

## Progression & Regression

**Concept**: Every drill has multiple difficulty levels. Not one-size-fits-all.

**Why it matters**: Rugby teams have mixed ability. A drill that stretches a beginner overwhelms them; a drill designed for elite players bores novices.

**Structure**:
- **Regression** (easier): Reduced complexity, slower pace, fewer decisions, more space
- **Base version** (target): Designed for majority of squad
- **Progression** (harder): Added complexity, faster pace, more decisions, less space

**Example**: A "support play" drill might have:
- Regression: 2v1 in open space, defender doesn't tackle hard
- Base: 3v2 with tackle context, realistic speed
- Progression: 4v3 with scramble chaos, pressure decision-making

**Application in coaching-animator**: Flag drills with progression/regression variants. Coaches could visualize the difficulty gradient, link progressions together for a season's development arc.

## Tell, Sell, Ask, Delegate (TSAD)

**Framework**: Four coaching approaches with different purposes and effectiveness.

**Tell** (Direct instruction)
- Coach demonstrates and explains
- Best for: Safety, rules, initial technical teaching
- Risk: Passive learning, low ownership

**Sell** (Persuasive instruction)
- Coach explains the *why* behind the technique
- Best for: Buy-in, understanding context, motivation
- Risk: Still coach-directed

**Ask** (Guided discovery)
- Coach poses questions; players discover answers
- Best for: Deeper learning, problem-solving, engagement
- Risk: Takes longer, requires skill to scaffold

**Delegate** (Autonomous learning)
- Coach sets problem; players solve independently
- Best for: Leadership, resilience, intrinsic motivation
- Risk: Requires maturity and safety culture

**Progression in coaching**: Good coaches move left-to-right (Tell → Sell → Ask → Delegate) as player capability grows.

**Application in coaching-animator**: Coaches could annotate drills with coaching approach (e.g., "this is a Tell drill for beginners, scale to Ask once comfortable"). Over time, they see their coaching evolution.

## Aspiration: Coaching-Animator as a Learning Platform

**Vision** (Phase 4+): Transform from animation tool to integrated coaching education system.

**Elements**:
1. **Drill library with pedagogical metadata** — Every animation tagged with APES alignment, progression variants, recommended coaching approach
2. **Coaching guides linked to drills** — Short contextual articles: "How to use Ask with this drill," "Why this regression matters"
3. **Session design tool** — Build a week's training: select drills, ensure APES balance, see progression arc
4. **Coaching reflections** — Post-session notes: "Which APES did we hit? What coaching approach worked? Progression thoughts?"
5. **Analytics for coaches** — Over a season: "Your drills trend toward Ask/Delegate (good), but low Safe/Enjoyable signals" → coaching advice

**Phase 2 Taster** (optional): Single info page (e.g., `/help/apes`) explaining one framework with a worked example from the gallery.

**Why it matters**: Grassroots coaches are busy, self-taught, sometimes under-resourced. Embedding pedagogy into the tool normalizes best practices and gives them confidence.
